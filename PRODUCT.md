# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The 12 members of the Blake's Shoes fantasy football league. They pull the site up casually on their phones: checking standings, settling an argument about who won what year, looking up a rule during draft season, or bragging about a trophy. There are no accounts, no logins, no notifications. Nobody is "doing work" here, they're browsing something that belongs to them.

## Product Purpose

A trophy case and record book for a 14-season friend-group fantasy football league. It exists to make the league's history feel real and worth remembering: who won, who's still chasing a first title, whose team name is the funniest bit that's stuck around since 2012. Success is a champion screenshotting the champion band for the group chat, and a last-place finisher wincing when they see their name on Hall of Champions (or, this year, not on it).

## Brand Personality

Gritty sports-broadcast. Confident, a little loud, ESPN-trophy-case energy with a wink of macho trash-talk (team names like "Not Popular Boys" and "Jew Crew" are part of the bit, not accidents). Editorial-magazine structure (masthead, banded sections, big Bebas Neue numerals) rather than app chrome. Not corporate, not precious.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS/startup landing page: no hero-metric-with-gradient-card template, no soft pastel dashboard look, nothing that reads as a templated Notion/Linear clone.
- Corporate or stock-photo sports software: avoid the bland ESPN-clone or league-management-tool look, dated Yahoo Fantasy chrome, ad-heavy stock layouts.

## Design Principles

- Broadcast, don't dashboard: every section should read like a graphic a sports network would put on screen, not a settings panel.
- Photos are the trophy case: the owners' real faces and the champion's photo are the emotional payload of the site, prominence for them is never wasted space.
- One system, no exceptions: every page pulls from the same fixed token set (colors, type, radius-0-except-avatars) established by the existing Broadcast design system, a "polish pass" tunes within it rather than introducing a parallel style.
- Mobile is the real venue: most visits happen on a phone mid-argument or mid-draft, so the mobile layout is the primary design, not a shrunk desktop one.
- Earn every ounce of chrome: this is a hobby site for 12 people, not a product with a roadmap, so restraint and craft matter more than features or configurability.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard good practice: reasonable contrast, legible type sizes, keyboard and touch-friendly targets. No formal WCAG level target and no specific known user needs beyond that for this group.
